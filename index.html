Question 2 • Short Answer Clinical Correlation

Cellular Mechanism of Hyperkalemic QRS Widening

Clinical Scenario: A 62-year-old male with end-stage renal disease misses two consecutive hemodialysis sessions. Serum potassium is 7.8 mEq/L. An ECG reveals tall peaked T waves, loss of P waves, and marked QRS prolongation.

Prompt: Explain the cellular mechanism by which severe hyperkalemia impairs myocardial conduction velocity and broadens the QRS complex.

High-Yield Board Model Answer:
  • Resting Potential Shift: Elevated extracellular K+ shifts the potassium Nernst potential (EK) in a positive direction, partially depolarizing resting membrane potential (e.g., from -90 mV toward -65 mV).
  • Voltage-Gated Na+ Channel Inactivation: Sustained baseline depolarization traps a large fraction of fast voltage-gated Na+ channels in their closed, inactivated state.
  • Reduced dV/dtmax: During excitation, fewer Na+ channels are available to open, markedly diminishing the Phase 0 upstroke velocity (dV/dtmax).
  • ECG Manifestation: Slower intraventricular electrical conduction velocity across myocytes prolongs total ventricular depolarization time, widening the QRS complex.
Question 3 • Integrative Long-Answer Essay

Hemodynamic & Neurohumoral Compensation in Acute Hemorrhage

Clinical Scenario: A 24-year-old trauma victim experiences a femoral artery laceration and loses 1200 mL of whole blood within 10 minutes.

Prompt: Detail the hemodynamic sequence immediately following blood loss and describe the compensatory neurohumoral reflex responses that act to restore mean arterial pressure and cardiac output. Address:

  1. Primary shifts in the Guyton cardiac and vascular function curves.
  2. The arterial baroreceptor reflex arc and efferent autonomic adjustments.
  3. Endocrine compensation via RAAS and vasopressin.

Complete Grading Rubric & Breakdown:
1. Guyton Hemodynamic Analysis:

Blood loss acutely lowers mean systemic filling pressure (Pmc), shifting the vascular function curve leftward/downward in parallel. Right atrial pressure and venous return decline, reducing end-diastolic volume (preload). Via the Frank-Starling mechanism, stroke volume and cardiac output drop, triggering immediate arterial hypotension.

2. Arterial Baroreflex & Efferent Adjustments:

Reduced pulsatile stretch in the carotid sinus (CN IX) and aortic arch (CN X) lowers afferent firing to the medullary NTS. This suppresses cardioinhibitory vagal centers and disinhibits the rostral ventrolateral medulla (RVLM):

  • Cardiac β1 activation: Increases heart rate, AV conduction, and inotropy (rotating cardiac function curve upward/counterclockwise).
  • Vascular α1 activation: Constricts resistance arterioles (increasing SVR) and venoconstricts capacitance reservoirs to restore effective circulating volume.
3. Endocrine Compensation:

Renal hypoperfusion and renal β1 activation release renin, driving Angiotensin II production (α1-like arteriolar constriction and aldosterone-mediated Na+/fluid retention). Unloaded baroreceptors also stimulate posterior pituitary ADH (vasopressin) release, activating vascular V1 receptors (vasoconstriction) and collecting duct V2 receptors (water retention).
